# Creek Watch demo: voice-over script (Alec)

**You're recording the narration yourself.** It replaces the text-to-speech voice. JP puts your audio over the screen recording.

**Length rule (contest):** the finished video must be **between 3:00 and 5:00**. This script is about **4:15 of speech** at a relaxed pace (140 words a minute). With pauses it lands at about **4:35–4:55**, close to the limit.
- **Time your first full read.** If it runs **over 4:45**, cut the lines marked *(optional)*. That brings it to about 4:15 finished.
- If it's **under 3:15**, slow down. Don't add words.

---

> ### 🎙️ Recording tips
> - **One quiet room.** Turn off fans and the fridge hum if you can. Soft furnishings (a bedroom or a closet full of clothes) sound better than a kitchen.
> - **Phone about 15 cm (6 inches) from your mouth.** Hold it a little to the side, not straight in front, so your "p" and "b" sounds don't pop.
> - **Stay silent for 2 seconds at the start of every file** before you speak, and for 1 second at the end.
> - **One file per section,** named **01.wav, 02.wav … 08.wav**. Any voice-memo app is fine; if it saves .m4a, that's OK too, just keep the numbers.
> - **Made a mistake?** Re-record only that section. You don't need to restart the whole thing.
> - **Read it like you're telling a friend,** not like a news anchor. Smile a little; people can hear it.
> - Each **`/`** is a small breath or pause.

**Say these the local way:**
- **Nevada** (City / County): *neh-VAD-uh*, not "neh-VAH-duh".
- **Yuba:** *YOO-buh*.
- **Algae:** *AL-jee*.
- **E. coli:** *ee-KOH-lye*.
- **CAP:** just say "cap", like a hat.

**Fill in from the app on the day:** where you see **[N]**, **[score]** or **[band]**, open the app just before recording and **read the real number or word you see**. Don't guess.
- **[N]** is the number of reports on the map.
- **[score]** and **[band]** come from the Wolf Creek card on the **Creeks** page, for example "89" and "good".

## 01: Hook and tracks (about 0:00–0:26)
**SHOW:** your creek video (water moving), then the title card "Creek Watch, creekwatch.realm.watch".
**SAY:**
> This is Wolf Creek, / in Grass Valley, California. / It runs right through town, / past parks, / houses, / and streets. / But if you asked people here, / "Is the creek healthy today?" / almost nobody could tell you.
>
> This is Creek Watch. / It's built for Track 2, / Data to Insight, / with parts of Track 1, / Track 6, / and Track 7.

## 02: The problem (about 0:26–0:51)
**SHOW:** your Sunday photos: a wide shot of the creek, a close-up of the water, anything notable.
**SAY:**
> Wolf Creek has no live stream gauge at all. / Deer Creek's only gauge / is twenty-two kilometres downstream, / below a lake. / And a gauge can't see trash, / algae, / or a strange smell. / People walking by can. / But there's no easy way to tell anyone, / and the warnings that do exist / are scattered across different government websites.

## 03: The two-minute report, plus cleanups (about 0:51–1:54)
**SHOW:** phone-sized screen recording of a full report: photo, place, the six questions, the trash step with **"I picked it up"** and the bag counter, then the confirmation and a badge. *(New footage: see the SHOW list at the end.)*
**SAY:**
> Creek Watch fixes that / with a two-minute report / anyone can do on their phone. / No app store. / Just open the website.
>
> Take a photo. / The app uses your location / to pick the nearest spot on the creek. / Then you answer simple questions / with big buttons: / What colour is the water? / Any algae? / Any trash? / How is it flowing? / Does it smell? / Any dead fish?
>
> If you see trash / and you can safely pick it up, / tap "I picked it up", / and add how many bags if you like. / The app reminds you / to leave needles, / chemicals, / and big items alone, / and just report them. / Helpers earn small badges, / like Creek Helper / and Trash Hero. / The badges stay on your own phone. / No account.
>
> *(optional)* Your photo's hidden location data is removed, / and your name is optional.

## 04: Real reports on the map (about 1:54–2:17)
**SHOW:** the Map page zoomed to Grass Valley and Nevada City; tap a pin to show a real report with its photo.
**SAY:**
> On Sunday morning / I went out to Wolf Creek and Deer Creek / and filed real reports. / There are [N] on the map right now. / Every report shows up as a pin, / coloured by how healthy that spot looks. / Tap one, / and you see the photo / and exactly what was reported.

## 05: Dashboard, score and early warning (about 2:17–2:59)
**SHOW:** the Creeks page: the Wolf Creek card, the score, the list of reasons, the gauge and weather box, and the cleanup counter if it's showing.
**SAY:**
> Each creek gets a health score / from zero to a hundred. / Right now Wolf Creek is at [score], / which is "[band]". / And the score explains itself. / Every reason is written in plain words, / like recent reports of cloudy water, / or how much rain fell yesterday.
>
> It also gives early warnings. / Heavy rain plus brown water / means "watch for runoff". / Lots of algae in warm weather / means "watch for an algae bloom". / Dead fish, / or a chemical or sewage smell, / is an alert.
>
> *(optional)* Each card also counts / the cleanups people have reported.

## 06: Water alerts and push notifications (about 2:59–3:44)
**SHOW:** the Alerts page (scroll the list; tap one alert and show its "official source" link), then the **Get alerts** screen choosing a creek, then the welcome notification "Creek Watch alerts are on". *(New footage.)*
**SAY:**
> Creek Watch also puts water warnings / in one place. / It checks seven outside sources, / like the National Weather Service, / state reports of sewage spills / and algal blooms, / California's fish advisories, / and volunteer water tests, / plus its own warnings / from people's reports. / Every official alert / links to its source.
>
> You can turn on alerts / for just the creeks you care about. / A welcome notification / confirms it's working.
>
> *(optional)* For other systems, / the alerts are also published / in CAP, / a standard format / that public warning systems use.
>
> For real emergencies, / the app points you to / Nevada County Alerts / and 911.

## 07: The data (about 3:44–4:11)
**SHOW:** the About page (the source list and links), then the Wolf Creek card's **"Past study · 2024"** panel with its chart.
**SAY:**

>
> In 2024, / the regional water board studied bacteria / along Wolf Creek, / and you can see that history here too.

> *(optional)* All the code is open source.
