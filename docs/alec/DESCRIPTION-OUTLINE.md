## 1. Title and one-line summary (1–2 sentences)
- **Prompt:** Creek Watch is a ___ that lets ___ do ___ so that ___.
- Example to rework: "Creek Watch is a phone app that lets anyone standing at a creek report what they see in about two minutes, and turns those reports plus public stream and weather data into a creek-health score and early warning."

## 2. Track (2–3 sentences)
- **Primary: Track 2, Data-to-Insight.** We turn citizen reports into a map, a dashboard and a health score that explains itself.
- **Also Track 1, Citizen Science UX:** a guided report flow in plain words, with big buttons.
- **Also Track 6, Resilience Informatics:** an early-warning signal, such as heavy rain plus brown water, which means watch for runoff.
- **Prompt:** in one sentence, which of these matters most to you, and why?

## 3. The problem (3–4 sentences)
- **Prompt:** What did you notice at Wolf Creek or Deer Creek on Sunday? Start with something you saw yourself.
- **Prompt:** Why is it hard for regular people to know whether a creek is healthy today? (Gauges measure water flow, not trash or algae. Few people know where or how to report.)
- **Prompt:** Who finds out late when something goes wrong? Think about people, dogs and wildlife.
- ⚠️ No numbers unless JP gives you a source link.

## 4. Our solution (4–5 sentences)
- **Report:** a photo plus 6 quick taps (colour, algae, trash, flow, smell, dead fish). Your location fills in automatically.
- **Map:** every report is a pin, coloured by how healthy the creek looks.
- **Dashboard:** one card per creek with a 0–100 score, the reasons behind it in plain words, and the latest stream-gauge and weather readings.
- **Early warning:** rules flag a "watch" or "alert", for example dead fish or a chemical smell means alert.
- **Prompt:** which part surprised you most when you used it?

## 5. Who it's for (2–3 sentences)
- Residents, walkers, students and volunteer creek groups who are already at the water.
- Teachers, as a field-trip activity.
- City and county staff, and researchers, who want earlier signals between official samples.
- **Prompt:** describe one real person who would use it, without naming them.

## 6. Impact: One Health (3–4 sentences)
- One Health means the health of the creek, the animals and the people are linked.
- **Prompt:** give one example of each:
  - creek, such as sediment after a storm;
  - animals, such as fish, birds, or dogs that swim;
  - people, such as kids playing in the water, or drinking-water sources downstream.
- **Prompt:** what changes if lots of people report every week instead of a few experts a few times a year?

## 7. What we did this weekend (2–3 sentences)
- Built it, tested it on real phones, and filed the first real reports from the creeks on Sunday morning.
- **Prompt:** how many reports did you file, and at which spots? (Use the real count.)

## 8. What's next (2–3 sentences)
- Possible next steps: partner with local creek volunteer groups, add more creeks, send alerts by text, translate it into Spanish, and share data in standard formats.
- **Prompt:** pick the one you'd do first and say why.
